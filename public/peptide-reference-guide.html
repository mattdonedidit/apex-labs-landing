Introduction & Scope

Welcome to the APEX LABS Research Peptide Reference Guide.

This document provides comprehensive, evidence-based information on 10 widely-studied peptide compounds used in biomedical and cellular research. Each profile includes structural background, documented research applications, dosage ranges for research contexts, stability information, and storage guidelines.

Intended Audience: Qualified researchers, academic institutions, biotech professionals, and individuals with formal training in biochemistry, cell biology, or related fields.

What This Guide Covers:

  • Structural and biochemical origin of each compound
  • Documented research areas and cellular mechanisms
  • Dosage ranges in a research-only context
  • Half-life, duration of action, and stability profiles
  • Proper storage and handling protocols
  • Compliance and safety information

What This Guide Does NOT Provide:

  • Medical advice or treatment recommendations
  • Instructions for human use or consumption
  • Claims about disease prevention, treatment, or cure
  • Regulatory approval status for human medicine

Research Context Only: All dosage information provided refers strictly to in vitro and in vivo research applications. Researchers are responsible for adhering to institutional review boards (IRBs), animal care committees (IACUCs), and all applicable regulations in their jurisdiction.

1. BPC-157 (Body Protection Compound)

BPC-157

Full Name: Body Protection Compound-157
Classification: Synthetic pentadecapeptide
Structural Origin & Background

BPC-157 is a synthetic peptide derived from body protection compounds found in gastric juice. It consists of 15 amino acids and has been extensively studied in both in vitro and animal models for its protective and regenerative properties.

Documented Research Areas
  • Tissue repair and regeneration (muscle, tendon, ligament)
  • Angiogenesis (blood vessel formation)
  • Neuroprotection and neuroplasticity
  • Gastrointestinal mucosa protection
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
10–1000 nM concentrations
Animal Studies:
1–10 μg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~6–8 hours (in vivo)
Duration:
Effects observed 24–72h post-administration
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C (refrigerated). Protect from light. Reconstituted solutions stable for 7–14 days when kept refrigerated. For long-term storage, freeze at –20°C or below.

2. TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4 Analogue)

TB-500

Full Name: Thymosin Beta-4
Classification: 43-amino acid peptide
Structural Origin & Background

TB-500 is a naturally occurring peptide found in thymus tissue and serum. Research versions are synthetic analogues of thymosin beta-4, known for immune modulation and tissue repair properties. It regulates actin dynamics and cellular migration.

Documented Research Areas
  • Cell migration and tissue remodeling
  • Angiogenesis (neovascularization)
  • Collagen deposition and wound healing
  • Myogenic differentiation
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
1–100 nM concentrations
Animal Studies:
2–20 mg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~2 hours (in vivo)
Duration:
Biological effects persist 48–96h despite short half-life
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C in sealed vials. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles. Reconstituted solutions stable for 10–14 days refrigerated. Lyophilized (powder) form stable for 24+ months at 2–8°C.

3. Tirzepatide (GIP/GLP-1 Receptor Agonist)

Tirzepatide

Full Name: Tirzepatide (GIP/GLP-1 Receptor Agonist)
Classification: 39-amino acid peptide analog
Structural Origin & Background

Tirzepatide is a dual glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist. It activates both GIP and GLP-1 signaling pathways simultaneously, making it a unique compound in metabolic research.

Documented Research Areas
  • Glucose homeostasis and insulin secretion
  • Metabolic regulation and weight dynamics
  • Incretin system function
  • Cellular signaling and energy metabolism
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
0.1–100 nM concentrations
Animal Studies:
0.1–3 mg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~5 days (in vivo, extended release)
Duration:
Steady-state effects at 4–5 week intervals
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C in original container. Protect from light and freezing. Once reconstituted, stable for 28 days refrigerated. Pre-filled pens should not be frozen.

4. Retatrutide (GLP-1/GIP/Glucagon Receptor Agonist)

Retatrutide

Full Name: Retatrutide (Triple Receptor Agonist)
Classification: Synthetic peptide, 34 amino acids
Structural Origin & Background

Retatrutide is a novel triple receptor agonist that activates GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors simultaneously. This tri-agonist approach represents advanced metabolic research into multi-pathway regulation.

Documented Research Areas
  • Metabolic regulation across multiple pathways
  • Glucose homeostasis and gluconeogenesis
  • Lipid metabolism and mitochondrial function
  • Multi-tissue endocrine signaling
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
0.01–10 nM concentrations
Animal Studies:
0.05–2 mg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~6–7 days (in vivo)
Duration:
Cumulative effects observed over 2–4 weeks
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C. Protect from freezing and light exposure. Reconstituted solutions remain stable for 14–21 days under refrigeration. Do not reuse once opened.

5. NAD+ (Nicotinamide Dinucleotide)

NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide)

Full Name: β-Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide
Classification: Cofactor, nucleotide
Structural Origin & Background

NAD+ is an essential coenzyme found in all living cells. It plays a central role in metabolic redox reactions and serves as a substrate for NAD+-consuming enzymes including sirtuins and PARPs. Research-grade NAD+ is synthetically produced and used in various cellular studies.

Documented Research Areas
  • Mitochondrial function and ATP production
  • Sirtuins activation and cellular aging models
  • DNA repair pathways (PARP signaling)
  • NAD+ metabolism and metabolic adaptation
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
100 μM–10 mM concentrations
Animal Studies:
250–1000 mg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~30–60 minutes (in vivo)
Duration:
Acute effects; chronic studies use repeated doses
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C away from light. NAD+ is sensitive to hydrolysis; use fresh solutions when possible. Unopened vials stable for 12–24 months. Reconstituted solutions should be used within 24 hours.

6. PT-141 (Melanotan II Analogue)

PT-141

Full Name: Bremelanotide (PT-141)
Classification: 7-amino acid peptide, melanocortin agonist
Structural Origin & Background

PT-141 (Bremelanotide) is a melanocortin-1A receptor agonist derived from alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH). It was developed as a research compound for studying neuroendocrine signaling and melanocortin pathways.

Documented Research Areas
  • Melanocortin receptor signaling
  • Neuroendocrine function and hormone regulation
  • Pigmentation and melanocyte biology
  • Central nervous system receptor pharmacology
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
1–100 nM concentrations
Animal Studies:
0.1–1 mg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~2–3 hours (in vivo)
Duration:
Peak effects at 30–60 minutes post-injection
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C in sealed vials. Protect from light and moisture. Reconstituted solutions stable for 7–10 days refrigerated. Lyophilized form stable for 18+ months.

7. Sermorelin (GHRH Analogue)

Sermorelin

Full Name: Sermorelin Acetate (GHRH 1–29 analogue)
Classification: 29-amino acid peptide
Structural Origin & Background

Sermorelin is a synthetic analogue of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH). It consists of the first 29 amino acids of the natural 44-amino acid GHRH and is used in research examining growth hormone secretion and somatotropic axis signaling.

Documented Research Areas
  • Growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor signaling
  • Somatotropic axis function
  • Anterior pituitary hormone secretion
  • Aging models and neuroendocrine regulation
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
0.1–100 nM concentrations
Animal Studies:
1–10 μg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~8–10 minutes (in vivo)
Duration:
GH response peaks at 30–45 minutes post-injection
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C. Protect from freezing (unless lyophilized). Reconstituted solutions stable for 7–14 days refrigerated. Avoid exposure to heat and direct light.

8. Ipamorelin (GH Secretagogue)

Ipamorelin

Full Name: Ipamorelin (Growth Hormone Secretagogue)
Classification: Pentapeptide
Structural Origin & Background

Ipamorelin is a pentapeptide growth hormone secretagogue (GHS) that selectively stimulates GH release through ghrelin receptor signaling. Unlike other GHS compounds, ipamorelin shows minimal effects on cortisol and prolactin, making it valuable for selective GH axis research.

Documented Research Areas
  • Ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a) agonism
  • Growth hormone secretion and regulation
  • Selective GH axis stimulation without hormone dysregulation
  • Metabolic research and muscle protein synthesis pathways
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
0.1–100 nM concentrations
Animal Studies:
0.5–5 μg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~2–3 hours (in vivo)
Duration:
GH peak at 30–60 minutes; effects persist 2–4 hours
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C. Protect from freezing (unless lyophilized). Reconstituted solutions stable for 7–10 days refrigerated. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles.

9. HGH (Human Growth Hormone)

HGH (Human Growth Hormone)

Full Name: Somatotropin (191-amino acid peptide)
Classification: Recombinant polypeptide hormone
Structural Origin & Background

HGH is a 191-amino acid peptide hormone produced by the anterior pituitary gland. Research-grade HGH is manufactured using recombinant DNA technology. It plays crucial roles in growth, metabolism, and cellular turnover, making it extensively studied in endocrinology and aging research.

Documented Research Areas
  • Growth hormone receptor (GHR) signaling
  • Cellular proliferation and differentiation
  • Metabolic regulation and lipid metabolism
  • Protein synthesis and cellular aging models
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
1–1000 ng/mL concentrations
Animal Studies:
1–10 μg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~15–20 minutes (in vivo)
Duration:
Hormonal effects persist 6–12 hours; IGF-1 effects longer-term
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C. Protect from freezing of liquid form (unless specified lyophilized). Reconstituted solutions stable for 14–28 days refrigerated. Avoid shaking; roll gently to mix. Unopened vials stable for 24+ months.

10. IGF-1 LR3 (Insulin-Like Growth Factor-1 Long R3)

IGF-1 LR3

Full Name: Insulin-Like Growth Factor-1 Long R3 (70-amino acid peptide)
Classification: Recombinant growth factor analogue
Structural Origin & Background

IGF-1 LR3 is a modified analogue of insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1). It features an extended N-terminus and increased resistance to IGF-binding proteins, resulting in prolonged biological half-life compared to native IGF-1. Used extensively in cellular and tissue culture research.

Documented Research Areas
  • IGF-1 receptor (IGF-1R) signaling and proliferation
  • Myogenic differentiation and muscle growth
  • Neuronal survival and neuroprotection
  • Cellular metabolism and protein synthesis
Research Dosage Ranges (in vitro/in vivo)
In Vitro:
1–100 ng/mL concentrations
Animal Studies:
20–100 μg/kg per injection
Pharmacokinetics
Half-life:
~12–24 hours (in vivo, extended due to LR3 modification)
Duration:
Sustained effects over 24–48 hours per injection
Storage & Stability

Store at 2–8°C. Protect from freezing and light. Reconstituted solutions stable for 7–14 days refrigerated. Extended half-life analogue may require slightly more careful handling than native IGF-1. Avoid vigorous agitation.
